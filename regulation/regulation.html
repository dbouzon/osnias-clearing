Regulatory Positioning

A functional description before legal qualification

This page describes the operational perimeter of the Osnias architecture and the intended roles of its tokens. It does not claim a universal legal classification. Regulatory treatment may differ by jurisdiction, activity, counterparties and implementation. Osnias Clearing is submitting jurisdiction-specific requests for guidance to relevant authorities and records the status of those approaches publicly where appropriate.

01 · Functional perimeter

What Osnias does — and what it does not do

The architecture is designed around a narrow functional distinction: governance, clearing and third-party lending are separate activities. The regulatory analysis should therefore begin from the actual function performed by each component rather than from the generic fact that blockchain tokens are used.

Clearing

Clearing & settlement

ORUSD and OEURO are designed as restricted clearing-register tokens used for direct settlement according to explicit protocol rules.

Governance

Token governance

OSNIAS is designed as a governance token with a distinct economic and voting function. Governance and clearing remain separate technical domains.

Lending

Independent partners

Lending, collateral custody, risk management and credit decisions remain within independent partner infrastructures.

Functional statement. Osnias Clearing does not collect customer deposits, does not issue credit, does not create leverage and does not operate a traditional banking balance-sheet activity.
02 · Token classification by function

Three tokens, three distinct functions

OSNIAS

Governance token. Fixed genesis supply, canonical governance state on Sei, EVM economic mobility and public technical documentation.

Governance
ORUSD

USD-denominated clearing-register token with restricted P2P circulation, explicit mint/burn, oracle attestation and manager-gated settlement.

USD clearing
OEURO

EUR-denominated clearing-register token with the same narrow clearing purpose and a distinct technical implementation and public audit reference.

EUR clearing
Important distinction. Shared use of blockchain infrastructure does not make these token classes economically identical. Governance rights, clearing representation and external lending services must be analyzed separately.

04 · Jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction review

Regulatory dialogue by authority

The same technical architecture may receive different legal treatment depending on jurisdiction, activity, counterparties and implementation. Osnias therefore uses separate factual submissions rather than assuming a universal classification.

France

A request for regulatory guidance has been submitted to the Autorité des marchés financiers (AMF). The submission presents the functional perimeter, token separation, testnet status and absence of customer-deposit, credit and protocol-leverage activity.

AMF — submitted

Andorra

A jurisdiction-specific request for guidance has been submitted to the Andorran financial authority using the same factual architecture and functional-separation principles.

AFA — submitted

Spain

A Spanish-language regulatory package has been prepared for submission. The objective is to obtain jurisdiction-specific guidance before any production deployment or local activity.

Spain — package prepared

Switzerland

A FINMA-oriented package is being prepared, subject to confirming the appropriate access and jurisdictional route for a project that is not yet operating from Switzerland.

FINMA — route under review

European Union

EU-level analysis remains relevant for MiCA, payment/e-money boundaries and the treatment of restricted clearing-register tokens. National submissions are used first to obtain concrete jurisdictional feedback.

EU perimeter — ongoing analysis

Other jurisdictions

Additional submissions may be opened where network integrations, partners or production activity create a material nexus. Each filing will remain factual and jurisdiction-specific.

Open review
Status principle. “Submitted” means that a request for guidance has been transmitted to the relevant authority. It does not mean that the authority has approved, authorized or classified Osnias Clearing.
05 · Regulatory method

Request for guidance, not self-classification

The purpose of the regulatory workstream is to provide each authority with the same technical facts while allowing the legal analysis to remain jurisdiction-specific. The project should not rely on a single global statement that a token or service is “regulated” or “unregulated”.

What will be submitted

Factual technical package

  • Token purpose and functional perimeter.
  • Source code and ABI references.
  • White papers and audit-reference documents.
  • Testnet deployment references.
  • Governance and privileged-role architecture.
  • Clearing / custody / lending separation.
  • Zenodo DOI permanent archives.
What will be asked

Jurisdiction-specific questions

  • How should each token be classified in the jurisdiction?
  • Which activities, if any, require authorization or registration?
  • Does the clearing-register model fall within payment, e-money or another regulated perimeter?
  • What obligations arise from third-party lending integrations?
  • What AML/KYC obligations attach to the relevant actor and activity?
  • Which disclosures should accompany public or partner deployment?
Legal-positioning principle. Technical architecture is described as fact. Legal classification is requested from, or analyzed against, the applicable regulatory framework in each jurisdiction.
06 · Transparency
