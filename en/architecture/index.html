Release 3.0 architecture

Release 3.0 · 26/10/2026

Working draft — target architecture, unaudited and subject to banking, contractual and regulatory validation.

Release 3.0 separates clearing computation on Sei, the bank interface in Go and funds held in dedicated bank escrow accounts. Ethereum Controllers and stablecoins are removed from this architecture.

A portable Core, one canonical chain

Business logic uses standard EVM Solidity without Sei-specific libraries or precompiles. Sei EVM is the reference canonical chain; configurable adapters isolate network dependencies. The Core validates obligations and maintains positions, cycles and evidence hashes. It holds no funds and cannot debit an IBAN.

A Go gateway with separate services

The Sei Connector observes events and checks finality. The ISO 20022 Engine validates XML/XSD messages and bank-specific variants. The read-only Escrow Service verifies locked funds; the Settlement Service submits only authorised net orders. Bank Adapters support REST/HTTPS with mTLS and, where available, EBICS 3.0 TS. Reconciliation & Audit matches postings and retains the audit trail. PostgreSQL stores off-chain states; keys are protected according to bank requirements, including HSM/KMS where required.

Individual escrow and confidential account mapping

Each active Osnias-ID maps to an escrow account with a distinct IBAN, assigned to one company, one ISO 4217 currency and a 1W, 9W or 13W cycle profile. Funds are not pooled between companies. The Osnias-ID / IBAN / bank / account holder / currency / cycle mapping remains in a protected off-chain registry. No identifying IBAN, bank statement or invoice details are published in cleartext on Sei.

Evidence of locked funds, beyond an account balance

Before activating capacity Cᵢ, the Core validates authentic, current bank evidence linked to the correct Osnias-ID and cycle. It establishes the amount actually locked, currency, validity period, restrictions and release conditions. Signatures, timestamps, expiry, unique references and replay protection are checked. A positive balance, revocable freeze or generic ISO 20022 message is insufficient.

Obligation admission and incident handling

Positions follow Pᵢ(t) = Cᵢ + Rᵢ(t) − Eᵢ(t). An obligation is admitted only if the position remains non-negative and all other checks pass. Missing, stale or inconsistent evidence blocks new obligations requiring that cover without cancelling previously accepted obligations. Clearing does not automatically discharge claims; that effect depends on the rulebook and applicable agreements.

From the active cycle to confirmed bank settlement

The bank locks funds before activation. During the cycle, the oracle can verify escrow at the Core’s request. At closing, positions are frozen; prescribed checks and disputes precede a deterministic batch by chamber, currency and cycle. Mandated gateways submit individualised instructions to banks. At most one net debit is generated per debtor account in the batch, subject to prescribed exceptions. Banks execute debits and credits; residual funds are released or reassigned only after bank confirmation and reconciliation.

Messages, channels and settlement evidence

pain.001 carries customer-to-bank payment initiation where contractually supported; pain.002 reports statuses. camt.053 and camt.054 support reconciliation of postings, with camt.052 for intraday reporting. pacs.008 belongs to the interbank flow. REST and EBICS carry exchanges through agreed interfaces: ISO 20022 is not a transport protocol. A positive receipt status alone does not prove final settlement.

Unique orders and evidence-based closing

Each instruction has a stable, unique reference bound to the frozen cycle state. If confirmation is missing, the Gateway retains PENDING_RECONCILIATION and queries the bank without creating a replacement order under another reference. Rejections, partial settlements and discrepancies remain exceptions. A cycle is marked settled only after sufficient bank evidence and reconciliation for all relevant accounts.

Federated nodes without Ethereum Controllers

Nodes handle onboarding, ERP interfaces, identity, client relationships and bank coordination in their jurisdictions. Release 3.0 replaces tokenised escrow, Ethereum Controllers and Ethereum/Sei reconciliation with bank escrow and attestations. The Core neither holds, issues nor transfers clearing tokens or stablecoins.
