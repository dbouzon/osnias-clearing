Architecture 3.0: clearing in your software, settlement executed by banks

Release 3.0, dated 26 October 2026, defines the target architecture for Clean Code refactoring and the POC: a standard Solidity EVM Core on Sei for clearing, a Bank Escrow Gateway written in Go for bank connectivity, and funds held in individual bank escrow accounts.

A Core on Sei that holds no funds

The Core validates obligations and records positions, cycles and proof hashes. It cannot debit an IBAN and does not hold, issue or transfer clearing tokens or stablecoins. Ethereum Controllers have been removed from Release 3.0.

Individual bank escrow accounts

Each active Osnias-ID maps to an escrow account with a distinct IBAN, assigned to one company, currency and cycle profile. Funds are not pooled across companies. The Osnias-ID/IBAN mapping remains protected off-chain.

Bank attestations of blocked funds

Clearing capacity relies on an authentic, up-to-date bank attestation of funds actually blocked, linked to the correct Osnias-ID and cycle. A credit balance alone is insufficient; the Escrow Service verifies the evidence on a read-only basis.

At cycle close, positions are frozen and checked. The Bank Escrow Gateway’s Settlement Service transmits only authorized net instructions; banks execute the debits and credits under mandate. ISO 20022 messages, including pain.001/pain.002 and camt.053/camt.054, support initiation, status reporting and reconciliation under the bank agreements. REST/HTTPS and, where supported by the bank, EBICS provide transport. Closure requires bank evidence and reconciliation: an acknowledgement alone does not prove final settlement.

Unaudited target architecture, subject to banking, contractual and regulatory validation. Clearing does not automatically discharge claims: that effect depends on the rulebook and applicable agreements.

02 · Founder, market and framework

Founder, market and regulatory framework

Project founder

Osnias Clearing was conceived, designed, organised and documented by its founder, Denis Bouzon, with 100 % self-funding to date. The project has now reached the stage where it needs support to complement the founder’s skills for the next steps: clean code rework, security, the multi-node demonstrator and audit.

Market: a solution that does not yet exist

Osnias offers companies a netting solution that does not exist today. Multilateral netting is a mechanism proven for decades, but reserved for interbank and market infrastructures: as an order of magnitude, CLS and Clearstream together process around EUR 4,300 billion of notional volume per day, i.e. more than EUR 1 million billion per year. Recurring commercial obligations between companies have no equivalent. The aim is therefore not to take a share of an existing market, but to open this mechanism to inter-company flows.

Regulatory framework and risks

The main risk is regulatory: qualification of the infrastructure and of the services provided. Pre-assessment is under discussion with the AMF; the files submitted to the CSSF (Luxembourg) and the AFA (Andorra) are awaiting discussion. The sector is waiting for this pre-assessment before any commitment: until then, exchanges with potential node operators remain exploratory.

The following design principles limit risk by construction (details: osnias-clearing.com/faq/):

PrincipleScope
Non-custodialThe Core holds no funds and cannot debit an IBAN. Banks hold funds in individual escrow accounts; the Go Bank Escrow Gateway transmits authorized net instructions under mandate.
No credit, overdraft or leverageExecution invariant: instantaneous net exposure ≤ available collateral; any obligation exceeding it is rejected by the code.
Individualised collateralNo common guarantee fund: risk stays with each participant.
No central novationOsnias is not designed as a CCP; distributed multilateral netting, final qualification to be determined with the authorities.
Node responsibilitiesAdmission, blocking and exit of participants are decided by the node manager, under its local framework.
No PFMI claimNo PFMI certification or compliance is claimed; the architecture refers to it for risk management.

Clean rework chain for the draft code

The tool matters less than the framing: each component is reworked in strict conformity with the technical specifications of Books I to VII.

  1. Cursor AI — assisted rewrite of the draft code from frozen specifications, consistent conventions, NatSpec documentation.
  2. Foundry — unit tests, fuzzing, invariant tests and multi-participant simulations.
  3. Slither — static analysis, detector review and remediation, dependency review.
  4. Verified testnet redeployment, then freeze of the baseline handed over to security hardening and audit.

SEP governance: milestone acceptance and payments

Phase 1 is envisaged under a French société en participation (SEP), a contractual joint venture. Partners can recommend architecture, ERP interfaces and test criteria from the Clean Code stage. Each package is split into milestones with deliverables, acceptance criteria and validation reports. Partners approve milestones and authorize progress payments under the SEP agreement. Costs already incurred remain exposed to development risk.

Phase 1 exit

A shared POC documented and evaluated with its partners, a technical assessment, remediation scope and Phase 2 specification. The operating company would be incorporated at this milestone, subject to contractual decisions and funding for its creation. Founding partners are expected to become shareholders under written agreements and the articles of association; technical participation alone does not automatically confer shares.

Phase 2 budget to be determined. Audit, regulatory advice, industrialization and deployment costs depend on scope, jurisdictions and authority requirements. Required authorizations must precede operation of the relevant activities.

Phase 3 · Commercialization

Integrate ERP software, activate nodes and commercialize Osnias Clearing

Following technical validation and required regulatory qualification, ERP vendors can provide B2B multilateral clearing through stable, documented APIs. Osnias Core maintains the common protocol; vendors develop connectors, invoice tracking and reconciliation; node operators handle admission, compliance and the services for which they are responsible. Banks execute net settlement under mandate. Osnias does not extend credit; legal and contractual due dates remain applicable.
