Book 3 · Release 1.3

Clearing Rules

Tokenless position ledger · Solvency control · Settlement by nodes

Book 3 defines the Osnias multilateral clearing rules. Release 1.3 simplifies the architecture: the clearing token is removed, each member's clearing capacity is recorded natively as a net position on the Sei ledger, backed 1:1 by its escrow on the Ethereum side, and the chamber's solvency is checked in real time by two oracles.

What changes in Release 1.3

Tokenless position ledger

The clearing token and MINT/BURN operations are removed. At cycle open, each member's position equals its escrowed amount; every accepted obligation atomically books −X on the debtor and +X on the creditor, without ever changing the system total.

Two-oracle solvency control

An escrow oracle on the Ethereum side totals the escrows and relays that total to Sei through the Controllers; a position oracle on the Sei side totals the net balances. The Sei net total must equal the Ethereum escrow total: the difference must be zero at all times.

Net position always positive

A member's net position can never be zero or negative: an ISO 20022 request is validated only if the net position is sufficient to cover it. As a debtor, each client is therefore exposed to its own collateral only.

Cycle closing calendar

P2P ends on Friday at 23:59 UTC; freeze and disputes on Saturday and Sunday; netting by the node manager on Monday; client escrow adjustment phase; then booking of the opening position. The escrow stays frozen for the whole cycle and no movement passes through an Osnias account.

A documented and auditable framework

  • Deterministic computation of end-user deltas and node positions, aggregation and ΣRN = 0 verification.
  • Dated statements published by nodes for their users and by Osnias for the nodes, dispute window and finalisation.
  • Inter-node settlement, four finality states, fail-closed exception handling and post-clearing mirror reconciliation.
  • Administration keys and powers, main risks, node licensing requirement, AML/CFT and the travel rule (TFR).

International reference frameworks, including the CPMI-IOSCO PFMI, are used as sources for analysis and design. Their inclusion, like the mention of the authorities potentially concerned (AMF, ACPR, Banque de France), does not prejudge the regulatory classification of Osnias or constitute a declaration of compliance.
