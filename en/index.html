Decentralised clearing infrastructure

Multi-node clearing network

Multilateral netting · Net settlement · Cross-jurisdiction clearing

Osnias Clearing is a multi-node clearing network project designed to net obligations within each node as well as obligations between nodes, with each node remaining under its own jurisdiction.

The network is aimed at middle-market players and above that trade on a recurring basis within the same area, across several areas or within the same industry chain. It seeks to reduce gross settlements by leaving only the net positions arising from economic cycles of 1, 4 or 13 weeks to be settled.

Assets remain under the control of clients and under the supervision of node operators, in accordance with their jurisdiction. By design, the clearing layer runs on its own blockchain infrastructure and remains strictly separate from, and sealed off from, the asset custody and settlement layer.

01 · Project overview

A clearing infrastructure for the multilateral netting of real economic exchanges

Osnias Clearing is designed to organise the multilateral netting of economic obligations between companies and institutions that trade with one another on a recurring basis. The aim is not to create a new means of payment, but to reduce gross settlements, calculate net positions and limit the liquidity required for final settlement.

The network is structured around local nodes. Each node brings together economic players operating within the same operational or legal environment. Mutual obligations are first netted within the node; only the residual balance is then exposed at inter-node level.

This structure supports use cases within a single economic area, across several jurisdictions, or in vertical industry chains where the same companies are in turn customers, suppliers or partners of one another. The model primarily targets middle-market players and above, for whom recurring flows and cross-obligations can justify netting organised over time.

The protocol relies on a common clearing ledger on Sei, which records obligations and calculates netting positions, while the assets intended for settlement remain separate at node level on Ethereum. This separation is structural: Osnias develops the clearing infrastructure but has no access to the escrowed assets used for settlement.

The 1-week, 4-week and 13-week cycles correspond to real economic horizons. They give the network the time needed to aggregate cross-obligations before calculating the net positions to be settled, first locally and then between nodes.

02 · Clearing model

A common ledger for inter-node and cross-jurisdiction clearing

Osnias Clearing organises a network of nodes connected to a common clearing ledger. Each node serves its own users, within its own legal and operational environment, while taking part in common netting with the other nodes.

Local netting

Within each node

The obligations of users of the same node are aggregated and netted locally before any inter-node exposure.

Inter-node netting

Across several nodes

After local netting, residual positions are aggregated at network level and netted between participating nodes.

Cross-jurisdiction

One common network, local frameworks

Each node remains a local entity subject to its own regulatory framework, while using a common clearing and messaging protocol.

03 · Functional architecture

Clearing on Sei, separate assets on Ethereum

The architecture strictly separates the calculation of clearing obligations from the custody of the assets used to settle them.

SEI

Common obligations ledger

Sei hosts the canonical clearing ledger, participant positions, cycles and the states required for netting calculations.

ETHEREUM

Escrow and settlement

Assets intended for settlement are held in escrow separately on Ethereum, at node level. They are not held by the clearing ledger.

OSNIAS

Separation by design

Osnias develops the clearing infrastructure on the Sei side. By design and by code, Osnias has no access to the assets held or escrowed on the Ethereum side.

04 · Economic cycles

1 week · 4 weeks · 13 weeks

Clearing cycles correspond to real economic horizons. They make it possible to aggregate reciprocal obligations before calculating net positions and settling them on a net basis, so as to reduce gross flows and the liquidity tied up.

Principle. The network seeks to generate liquidity savings by reducing the number and volume of gross settlements: netting first within each node, then between nodes, before net positions are settled.
