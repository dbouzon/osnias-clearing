Participant Security and Separation of Powers

Book 5 v2.1 describes a security architecture based on independent domains. For each participant, the design separates the operational EVM signing key, Sei authorization and a cold receiving address derived from a third seed. Osnias-ID binds these elements. Fund release relies on a composite condition, a pre-registered destination and replay protection: compromise of one operational key should not, by itself, be sufficient to divert escrowed funds.

Tuesday-to-Thursday Escrow Adjustment

After settlement on Monday, day 1 of the cycle, the participant may add funds or withdraw the releasable amount on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, days 2, 3 and 4, subject to applicable controls and outstanding obligations. Withdrawals are sent exclusively to the dedicated, pre-registered address, which is different from the escrow address. At the close of the Thursday window, escrow is locked for the active phase of the cycle. This lock is distinct from the end-of-cycle freeze of clearing positions.

The reference calendar is described in Book 4 — Cycles / Calendar. The exact Thursday UTC closing time remains to be specified, and the 1W testnet calendar must be explicitly aligned before the same sequence is applied.

Node Manager and Osnias Manager Keys

The node manager has a dedicated key derived from an independent seed, allowing it to act only on its assigned node. During the netting window on the first Monday of the cycle, the manager adjusts members’ accounts in the smart contracts according to the finalized net positions of the previous cycle. Depending on the node’s balance, the manager arranges transfer of the net amount due through the node account or waits for receipt of the net credit balance before finalizing the corresponding adjustments. Operations must remain restricted to authorized amounts, members and settlement destinations.

The Osnias manager uses a separate key to administer node parameters. Its functions include assigning node numbers and configuring per-node escrow limits in accordance with applicable regulatory requests. This role alone does not confer authority to dispose of escrowed assets. Parameter changes must be traceable; the treatment of limit reductions where funds are already escrowed remains to be specified.

Identity Lifecycle and Audit Readiness

The report also covers onboarding, voluntary identity renewal, off-boarding and terminal revocation. It examines registry consistency, cross-chain verification, key custody and rotation, compromise risks and failure scenarios. The control matrix distinguishes design requirements from implementation and testing evidence still pending.

This publication is an internal technical assessment and audit-readiness review. It is not an independent audit, formal verification or production-security certification. Security objectives must be assessed against the implemented code, adversarial testing, remediation and independent review.
