Understanding Osnias Clearing

An invoice gives rise to a payment obligation. When businesses are connected through purchases and sales, multilateral netting may reduce the amount of money that ultimately needs to move between them. Osnias aims to perform this calculation on a shared register without turning invoices into tradable assets or replacing banks as custodians of funds.

Clearing or netting identifies obligations that offset one another; the net position is the resulting balance for each business; settlement is the actual transfer of funds. These are separate processes.

Simplified example: A owes 100 to B, B owes 80 to C, and C owes 70 to A. Net positions are: A pays 30, B receives 20, and C receives 10. Gross obligations total 250, whereas 30 can settle the net positions in this example, provided the obligations are legally valid and admitted to the protocol.

Existing DLT clearing and settlement infrastructures: comparative overview

Financial market infrastructures already use distributed ledgers to record transactions, coordinate settlement and manage liquidity. These precedents demonstrate the feasibility of individual technical mechanisms, but do not establish an equivalent system for multilateral clearing of B2B trade payables and receivables. The calculation of net obligations, safeguarding of funds and legal finality of settlement must be assessed separately.

Osnias Clearing: proposed distinct architecture

Osnias aims to combine multilateral clearing of B2B trade obligations, synchronised collective cycles, a portable Solidity/EVM core and individually assigned bank escrow accounts, monitored through an independent bank oracle. Bank-specific functions and network adapters remain separate from core business logic. These are design objectives subject to pilot testing and regulatory assessment, not services represented as already authorised or in production.

The CLEAR 9W protocol envelope spans 63 calendar days between consecutive settlement points. Under Book IV, release 2.2, the commercial window begins on the Friday of week 1, leaving a maximum of 59 calendar days between admission and settlement. Each obligation must still meet its own contractual and statutory due date; otherwise admission is refused. 1W is for testnet validation, while 13W concerns legally eligible non-commercial obligations.

Comparative conclusion. Existing market systems demonstrate periodic netting, coordinated settlement and prefunding, individually or in combination. The potential differentiation of Osnias is their combined application to commercial B2B obligations and a 9W cycle. This selective review does not establish a claim of worldwide exclusivity.

What sets the Osnias proposal apart

The infrastructures reviewed primarily serve securities, foreign-exchange or fund-processing markets. Osnias targets a different use case: B2B trade obligations, coordinated cycles aligned with contractual due dates, individual bank escrow accounts and an independently verifiable banking oracle. This combination is proposed for pilot validation; worldwide novelty has not been established.

CLEAR 1W is for testing; CLEAR 9W is for eligible B2B trade obligations; CLEAR 13W is reserved for eligible non-commercial obligations. Under CLEAR 9W, settlement points are 63 days apart, but the maximum window from the opening of commercial submissions to settlement is 59 days. An invoice is not admitted when its legal or contractual due date precedes the scheduled settlement (Book IV, v2.2).

How responsibilities are separated

The standard Solidity/EVM Core calculates net positions and enforces common rules. Sei is a reference deployment, not a mandatory dependency of the Core. Node managers perform their contractual functions; banks maintain the accounts; the proposed banking oracle is intended to verify balances, activity and lock status independently. These responsibilities remain subject to contractual validation and applicable regulatory qualification.

Regulatory qualification and responsibilities

Multilateral Clearing and Services Actually Provided

The current architecture aims to record, aggregate and net obligations before settlement, then determine net balances. A position represented in a register must not be confused with the asset used to settle that balance. Position calculation, asset custody and execution of movements are therefore examined separately.

The node manager assumes the operational functions associated with its scope and authorizations. The operating company provides common rules, the register and technical protocol components. This allocation must be confirmed by contracts and actual permissions; it does not exempt any party from its own obligations.

The regulatory dossier must connect the services actually provided, the assets used and the responsible legal entity. Neither DLT use, the absence of direct custody by Osnias nor the term “node” alone determines the applicable regime. Material changes to functions or assets must be addressed in regulatory scoping discussions.

Osnias Clearing is presented as an infrastructure for calculating and clearing obligations, separate from custody and settlement functions. The clearing register on Sei records and reconciles positions; escrow and asset movements remain in a separate layer. Osnias does not hold users’ private keys or directly hold their funds.

Dialogue with AMF Innovation & Digital Finance concerns the functions described and their qualification under MiCA and the applicable French and European framework. Analysis must consider the assets used, technical permissions, mandates, contracts and responsibilities of each party, including any custody, transfers or payment services actually provided.

The project remains in development and on testnet; no definitive regulatory qualification is assumed. The historical memorandum and current documentation serve different purposes. The Architecture, Rules, Security and FAQ pages present the currently described operation and should be consulted alongside the historical document.

1. Historical document and scope of its interpretation

The AMF Memorandum v2.0 is retained as a historical documentary reference. It presents the technical facts, assumptions and qualification questions formulated at publication. Its French and English PDFs and DOI remain available. This page does not modify those files or present them as an exhaustive description of the current architecture.

2. Current architecture and responsibilities

In the current design, the canonical register on Sei records obligations, positions and clearing states. These positions are not standalone transferable or tradable digital assets. Settlement assets remain in an external escrow and settlement layer. Node managers and their providers act according to the services actually performed and the applicable authorizations.

3. Qualification questions and dialogue with authorities

Scoping seeks to examine the functions of the operating company, node managers and settlement providers under applicable law. Technical separation of layers is not, by itself, sufficient to determine regulatory status. Discussions with authorities are preliminary; they do not constitute authorization, permission for production deployment or a definitive qualification.
