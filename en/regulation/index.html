1. Technical and functional perimeter

The AMF Memorandum v2.0 first sets out the technical facts and functional perimeter of Osnias Clearing. It describes a canonical clearing register on Sei, clearing tokens governed by strict invariants, a non-custodial operating model, and an explicit separation between the calculation of obligations, the custody of assets and their final settlement.

2. Architecture, escrow and responsibilities

The document then details the external escrow architecture and the allocation of responsibilities between Osnias Clearing and node operators. It covers fail-closed controls, the 1:1 chamber-level invariant, the absence of a governance token, and the absence of asset custody by Osnias, in order to distinguish the clearing infrastructure from custody and settlement functions.

3. Regulatory qualification and AMF dialogue

Finally, the memorandum sets out the main regulatory qualification questions to be examined with the AMF as part of a preliminary regulatory dialogue. Its purpose is to obtain legal guidance on the system under MiCA and the applicable framework before developing a testnet pilot with a partner established in France, without prejudging any final legal qualification.

Digital clearing, MiCA and regulatory dialogue

Osnias Clearing is presented as an infrastructure for calculating and clearing obligations, distinct from custody and monetary settlement functions. This functional separation is central to the regulatory analysis: the clearing register on Sei records and consolidates positions, while settlement assets and escrow mechanisms remain external to the clearing core. The memorandum therefore describes a non-custodial architecture in which Osnias does not directly receive or hold participant funds.

The dialogue with AMF Innovation & Finance Digitale is intended to examine the system’s qualification under MiCA and the French and European framework applicable to digital-asset infrastructure. The approach is deliberately preliminary: it seeks to confront the technical facts, node-operator responsibilities, protocol invariants and the role of external escrow with the relevant legal analysis before any production deployment.

The phase described remains a testnet pilot and does not prejudge any final regulatory qualification. The public documentation, Zenodo DOI and French and English versions of the memorandum provide a stable working basis for regulators, technical partners, financial institutions, lawyers and auditors reviewing the clearing model, its operational boundaries and its relationship with custody and settlement functions.

Digital asset clearing infrastructure and multilateral netting

Osnias Clearing is positioned in the field of digital asset clearing infrastructure and multilateral clearing, rather than as a crypto-asset trading venue. Its architecture is designed to calculate, aggregate and net obligations before settlement, using a canonical clearing register on Sei and an explicit separation between clearing, custody and settlement. This places the project closer to financial market infrastructure and net-settlement concepts than to exchange or token-market infrastructure.

The model can also be described as non-custodial clearing and on-chain clearing: Osnias does not directly custody settlement assets, and escrow remains external to the clearing core. Multilateral netting, fail-closed controls and chamber-level invariants are intended to reduce gross settlement flows and support liquidity-efficient settlement, while keeping the protocol’s role distinct from that of node operators, custodians and settlement providers.

From a regulatory perspective, the dossier explores the intersection of MiCA, digital asset settlement, crypto-asset infrastructure and financial market infrastructure. The AMF Fintech dialogue focuses on the qualification of this hybrid architecture: a digital clearing register, external escrow, local node responsibilities and settlement outside the Osnias core. That distinction is central to avoiding the classification of the protocol as an exchange, custodian or stablecoin issuer solely because blockchain infrastructure is used.
