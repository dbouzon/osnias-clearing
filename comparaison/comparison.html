Architecture · Security · Compliance

Clearing vs Settlement Infrastructure

This page compares the architectural and security properties of Osnias Clearing with those of integrated on-chain settlement architectures. The comparison focuses on functional design, asset security, regulatory architecture and institutional compatibility — not on performance claims.

01 · Functional Design

What each system does

Clearing and settlement are distinct functions. Clearing identifies accepted obligations, calculates net positions and produces settlement instructions. Settlement discharges the residual obligation through asset transfer. Combining clearing and settlement within the same technical layer can concentrate custody, operational and counterparty dependencies. Osnias separates these functions by design.

Dimension Integrated Settlement Architecture Osnias Clearing
Primary function Direct transfer of settlement assets between counterparties Multilateral netting of accepted obligations — settlement executed by NNN nodes
Netting Typically bilateral or gross depending on system design ✓ Multilateral — all accepted obligations within a clearing room offset before settlement
Settlement execution Settlement execution may be integrated within the same technical stack Executed exclusively by NNN node operators under their own legal and regulatory framework
Clearing cycle Typically immediate or near-real-time — no fixed cycle boundary ✓ Defined cycles: 1, 4 and 13 weeks — each clearing room sets its own duration at creation
Obligation state transition Depends on the legal and technical model Accepted obligations and subsequent state changes are recorded against Osnias-IDs. Any legal novation, where applicable, remains governed by clearing-room documentation and applicable law
Scope of comparison: The comparison describes common characteristics of integrated on-chain settlement architectures. It does not purport to describe every settlement system, regulated FMI or institutional operating model.
Functional boundary: Osnias calculates and records the net settlement position. It does not perform settlement. Settlement occurs between and/or within NNN nodes according to their own settlement infrastructure, contracts and applicable regulatory framework.
02 · Asset Security

Who holds the assets and who can move them

Asset security depends on custody architecture. In a non-custodial system, the protocol cannot move client assets under any circumstance. Custody remains with the operator of each node, not with the clearing infrastructure.

Dimension Integrated Settlement Architecture Osnias Clearing
Custody model Protocol or smart contract may hold or route assets — custody risk depends on architecture ✓ Non-custodial — Osnias never holds collateral and never executes either leg of settlement
Collateral location May be held in protocol smart contract, liquidity pool or custodian arrangement ✓ Node-specific escrow contract — each NNN node operates its own audited escrow
Collateral integrity Depends on collateral and liquidity design ✓ Collateral invariant maintained — netting reduces gross flows but does not reduce escrow backing
Asset movement authority Movement authority depends on the settlement and custody model ✓ Only the NNN node operator authorises movement — Osnias has no access to escrow accounts
EOA restriction Smart-contract routing may be permitted depending on system design ✓ EOA-only — transfers restricted to externally owned accounts registered in the Osnias network
Default risk Default allocation depends on system rules and contractual arrangements Default management remains governed at NNN-node and clearing-room level under the applicable contractual and regulatory framework; Osnias does not mutualise participant losses
Architectural invariant: Osnias Clearing does not take custody of the settlement asset or collateral and does not execute either leg of settlement. Collateral remains subject to the node-specific escrow contract and the legal arrangements between the node and the participant.
03 · Standards and Regulatory Architecture

Regulatory architecture and institutional requirements

Institutional deployment requires an architecture capable of operating within established regulatory, governance and risk-management frameworks. Osnias therefore separates clearing, settlement, custody and credit functions by design, while leaving regulated activities and participant onboarding within the perimeter of the relevant NNN node operator.

Standard / Norm Integrated Settlement Architecture Osnias Clearing
CPMI-IOSCO Principles for FMIs Reference depends on operator, system documentation and regulatory status ✓ Declared reference in the Functional Position Paper
ISO 20022 messaging Implementation varies by system and operator ✓ Structured financial messaging aligned with ISO 20022 on Sei — Payment Request → Accept/Reject → Transfer
ISO 4217 currency codes Identifier and currency representation vary by system ✓ Embedded in Osnias-ID at account creation — immutable per clearing account
MOD-97 checksum Checksum model varies by identifier scheme ✓ Osnias-ID includes MOD-97 checksum — same standard as IBAN
EMIR (EU No 648/2012) Depends on the operator, jurisdiction and system documentation Referenced in the Functional Position Paper as part of the regulatory perimeter analysis; classification remains subject to applicable rulebook, contracts, governance and jurisdiction
FINMA guidelines Depends on the operator, jurisdiction and system documentation Swiss regulatory perimeter considered in the Functional Position Paper; classification remains subject to applicable Swiss law and the role performed by each operator
AML / KYC perimeter Responsibility varies by operator, architecture and regulatory perimeter ✓ Each NNN node retains responsibility for onboarding, AML and sanctions controls within its own regulatory perimeter
Identity confidentiality Identity and address models vary by architecture ✓ Osnias-ID separates clearing identifier from participant identity — mapping maintained outside canonical clearing layer
04 · Institutional Compatibility

Integration with regulated financial infrastructure

Integration with regulated financial infrastructure requires more than API access. It requires an architecture that preserves the functional boundaries of clearing, settlement, custody and credit, allowing connected operators to retain their own legal, compliance and operational perimeter.

Dimension Integrated Settlement Architecture Osnias Clearing
Clearing / settlement separation May be integrated within a single technical stack ✓ Strict functional separation — design principle, not a configuration option
Legal finality Legal finality depends on the applicable system rules, asset, contracts and governing law Legal finality arises at NNN node level under applicable system rules, contracts, settlement asset and governing law
Auditability Auditability depends on implementation, disclosure and operator controls ✓ All contracts deployed with creation hashes and compiler parameters — publicly verifiable on SeiScan and Etherscan Sepolia
No DEX / AMM / CEX May be integrated or adjacent depending on architecture ✓ Explicitly excluded — Osnias does not operate a DEX, AMM or CEX and does not provide leveraged trading or investment services
No token speculation Market circulation depends on the system and asset design ✓ ORUSD and OEURO not designed for market circulation, bridging or smart-contract routing
NNN node model Operator and counterparty model varies by system architecture ✓ Each NNN node is an independent settlement operator — compatible with existing institutional settlement infrastructure
Accounting treatment Accounting treatment depends on the relevant transaction, asset and jurisdiction Participants remain responsible for their own tax and accounting treatment — Osnias does not define accounting rules
Regulatory note: This page describes the intended allocation of technical functions. It is not a legal opinion and does not determine the regulatory classification of Osnias Clearing, ORUSD, OEURO, an NNN node, an escrow arrangement or a connected settlement service. Classification depends on the applicable rulebook, contracts, governance and actual operation of the network.
