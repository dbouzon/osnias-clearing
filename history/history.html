ONIAS III & ONIAS IV

From the Zadokite high-priestly tradition to a modern clearing architecture

Osnias Clearing takes its name and historical inspiration from the Oniad tradition associated with Onias III and Onias IV. Their world joined religious authority, public responsibility, stewardship of entrusted assets and the discipline of institutions over time. The modern protocol does not claim to reproduce an ancient institution: it adopts a contemporary clearing philosophy inspired by integrity, ordered time, reconciliation and periodic release.

01 · Onias III

Legitimacy, stewardship and the pursuit of justice

Onias III served as High Priest of the Jerusalem Temple in the second century BCE, approximately from 187 to 175 BCE. The historical tradition presents him as one of the last legitimate high priests of the Zadokite line. The Second Book of Maccabees is reported as describing him as a good man who sought justice.

Legitimacy

Zadokite continuity

Onias III belonged to the Oniad high-priestly family associated with the Zadokite line. His removal from office took place in a period when religious authority, political power and royal intervention were increasingly entangled.

Integrity

A reputation for justice

Ancient tradition preserved in the sources portrays Onias III as a good man committed to justice. This reputation is central to the historical memory adopted by Osnias Clearing.

Stewardship

Custody of entrusted wealth

As High Priest, Onias III was connected with the administration and protection of the Temple treasury, including funds deposited there by third parties. His office therefore carried both religious and civil responsibility.

Historical point. The surviving sources support Onias III's high-priestly office, his association with the Temple treasury, and his reputation for justice. They do not establish that he personally operated a modern-style clearing register.
02 · Onias IV

Leontopolis: continuity beyond Jerusalem

Onias IV, son of Onias III, left Judea for Ptolemaic Egypt. With the permission of Ptolemy VI, he established a temple in Egypt, traditionally dated to the middle of the second century BCE. The sanctuary became known as the Temple of Onias and gave the Oniad tradition an institutional continuation outside Jerusalem.

Transmission

From father to son

Onias IV inherited the prestige and expectations attached to the Oniad high-priestly family, even though the high priesthood in Jerusalem passed into other hands.

Leontopolis

A new institutional center

In Egypt, Onias IV founded a temple modeled on the Jerusalem sanctuary. The site associated with this temple is Tell el-Yahudiya, one of the places historically called Leontopolis.

Continuity

An Oniad community in Egypt

Sources describe an Oniad presence in Egypt that acquired political and military importance, with territory later remembered as the “land of Onias.”

03 · Historical Continuity

Jerusalem, Antioch and Egypt

c. 187–175 BCE

Onias III as High Priest

Onias III holds the high-priestly office in Jerusalem and is associated with the last known phase of recognized Oniad/Zadokite high-priestly legitimacy.

c. 171–170 BCE

Death of Onias III

After his deposition and exile, Onias III is killed near Antioch. His death becomes part of the memory of a displaced legitimate priestly order.

Mid-2nd century BCE

Onias IV in Egypt

Onias IV establishes the Temple of Onias in Ptolemaic Egypt, creating a durable institutional expression of the Oniad tradition outside Judea.

Until 73 CE

The Temple of Onias remains active

The Egyptian sanctuary survives for more than two centuries before being closed by order of Vespasian.

04 · Qumran & the Zadokite Memory

A historical connection — but not a proven direct transmission

The Qumran communities and the Dead Sea Scrolls preserve a strong Zadokite identity and a pronounced concern for ordered sacred time. Some twentieth-century scholars proposed identifying Onias III with the “Teacher of Righteousness” mentioned in the Qumran texts. That identification was once influential, but it has largely been abandoned and remains unproven.

Source discipline. The cited historical sources do not prove that Onias III or Onias IV personally transmitted the 364-day calendar, Jubilee rules, or a specific system of registers to Qumran. Osnias Clearing therefore treats this relationship as an intellectual and symbolic lineage, not as a demonstrated chain of documentary transmission.
05 · Time, Record and Word

The institutional meaning adopted by Osnias Clearing

In the ancient high-priestly world, the Temple was not only a sanctuary. It was also an institution in which entrusted wealth, obligations, public authority and sacred time converged. Osnias Clearing draws from this historical setting three modern principles.

Record

Keep an accountable register

A clearing system must preserve a traceable and reconcilable record of positions. The register is not the wealth itself; it records claims, movements and settlement states.

Word

Honor commitments

Clearing transforms reciprocal promises into an ordered process of reconciliation. The protocol gives participants time to perform, exchange and offset positions before final settlement.

Time

Give settlement a rhythm

Osnias Clearing treats time as part of economic architecture. Immediate computation does not require immediate final settlement.

06 · The Jubilee Clearing Legacy

A modern clearing rhythm inspired by periodic release

Osnias Clearing does not reproduce an ancient legal calendar. It translates the Jubilee idea into a modern operational principle: economic positions should have time to circulate and compensate, while the system periodically closes, reconciles and releases.

52 weeks Four 13-week clearing cycles form a 364-day operating year.
4 × 91 days Each cycle gives participants time to exchange, rebalance and compensate positions.
7th year A dedicated Jubilee Week closes the seven-year Clearing Epoch and releases the expiring framework.
Modern doctrine. Value is created by circulation, reciprocal exchange and compensation over time — not by the clearing token itself. The 91-day cycle is therefore a deliberate economic breathing space, not a technical delay.
07 · Why the Name Osnias Clearing?

Walking in the footsteps of an institutional tradition

The name Osnias Clearing is a deliberate reference to the Oniad tradition: integrity in office, responsibility for entrusted value, continuity of institutions and disciplined treatment of time. The protocol translates those themes into an EVM clearing architecture designed for the twenty-first century.

Ancient inspiration

Integrity, stewardship and ordered time

Onias III and Onias IV belong to an historical world in which priestly legitimacy, entrusted assets and institutional continuity were inseparable from the ordering of communal life.

Modern implementation

Registry, clearing and periodic reconciliation

Osnias Clearing uses blockchain infrastructure to record positions, reconcile them over time and settle residual balances through explicit clearing cycles while separating clearing from custody, lending and credit.
